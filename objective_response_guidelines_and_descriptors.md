A response that is completely free of mistakes, assumptions, and biases is called an objective, fact-based response. [1] 
Depending on the context, there are several precise words and phrases you can use to describe this standard of communication:
## Single-Word Descriptors

* Objective: Grounded strictly in reality and uninfluenced by personal feelings or guesses.
* Infallible: Absolutely trustworthy and incapable of being wrong.
* Irrefutable: So accurate and well-supported by evidence that it cannot be denied or disproved.
* Definitive: The final, authoritative answer that leaves no room for doubt or interpretation.
* Factual: Restricted strictly to measurable, verifiable facts. [2, 3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Idiomatic and Professional Phrases

* "Based purely on empirical data": Highlighting that the answer comes only from observed and recorded evidence.
* "A literal, baseline response": Stating exactly what is known without adding any extra interpretation or "reading between the lines."
* "With mathematical certainty": Used when a response is structurally or logically flawless, leaving zero room for error.
* "Stick to the brief": A professional phrase meaning to answer only what was asked without assuming any extra context.

## Adjectives for the Mindset Behind the Response
If you are describing the effort required to make a response this perfect, you would say it is rigorous, scrupulous, or meticulous. These words show that the person checked every single detail to completely weed out mistakes. [7] 
If you are trying to ask someone else for this type of response, you can simply tell them: "Please give me a strictly factual answer—leave out any speculation or assumptions."
